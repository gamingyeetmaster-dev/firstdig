# First Dig Growth System

## What growth is for

First Dig is a niche B2B prospecting product. The goal is not to build a large generic audience.
The goal is to put credible proof in front of the small number of Toronto businesses that can make
money by being first to a project or opening.

Primary funnel:

```
fresh public filing
      ↓
specific proof ("this happened before the obvious buying moment")
      ↓
qualified visitor
      ↓
sample / early-access request
      ↓
trial activation
      ↓
daily digest becomes a routine
      ↓
paid subscription
```

Instagram is a credibility and proof surface, not the primary acquisition engine. Direct, relevant
outreach and referrals should outperform broad social reach if the product is useful.

## Positioning

**Category:** filing intelligence for outbound teams.

**Promise:** see a project or opening while the vendor decision is still ahead.

**Mechanism:** read primary public filings every morning; classify, join and geocode them into
usable feeds.

**Contrast:** not a shared lead list, not scraped personal-data enrichment, not a marketplace.

**Products:**
- Teardown Feed — $79/month.
- Opening Soon — $99/month.
- Bundle — $149/month.
- 14-day trial, no card.

Never invent customers, testimonials, conversion rates, download counts, "exclusive" leads,
partnerships or demand. A filing proves that a filing exists; it does not prove that its subject
wants a vendor or that any First Dig user converted it.

## Short-form creative principles

Prior short-form research produced four useful mechanics that transfer to First Dig:

1. **Recognition** — the viewer understands the sales problem immediately.
2. **Tension** — timing creates a concrete advantage or loss.
3. **Reversal** — the record changes how the opening line is understood.
4. **Recipient** — a viewer knows which colleague or business owner should see it.

Do not copy the student-entertainment mix used for a consumer campus app. First Dig has a narrower,
professional audience. Transfer the attention mechanics, not the content genre.

A product demo and an entertaining/educational post have different jobs. Do not interrupt a useful
post with a feature list. A demo should make one benefit legible in seconds.

### Content pillars

**1. Real signal proof — highest priority**
One fresh record or tiny set of records, using only publishable source facts. Show when it filed,
what kind of project/opening it indicates and which vendor category would care. Never expose
personal contact details in a social post.

**2. Timing education**
Explain why a permit, licence application or fit-out can precede an obvious buying moment by weeks
or months. Teach the workflow, not generic "sales tips."

**3. Product demonstration**
Short native screen capture: filter → record → source trail → export/digest. One benefit per video.

**4. Build / methodology**
How First Dig joins filings, why two independent signals are stronger than one, how source freshness
is handled, and what the product deliberately does not enrich.

Avoid motivational founder content, generic AI/startup memes, daily feature dumps, fake urgency and
unrelated trend-jacking.

## Launch sequence

### Post 1 — brand thesis
Carousel: "The lead before it becomes a lead."
Purpose: explain the category and create a credible profile landing surface.

### Post 2 — real Teardown proof
Use a newly refreshed Toronto permit record. Structure:
- Hook: specific project type + timing.
- Evidence: filed date / stage / declared cost when present.
- Relevance: one line naming the trade categories that would act before construction advances.
- CTA: "See the feed → firstdig.app."

### Post 3 — real Opening Soon proof
Use one current multi-signal address. Show the signal stack and dates.
Do not call a business "confirmed" unless the underlying evidence actually supports that wording.

### Post 4 — 15–25 second product demo
Vertical 9:16. Start on the result, not on a logo animation. Show one task from start to finish.
Use plain high-contrast subtitles and cut only when new information appears.

Then let observed response determine the next batch.

## Publishing cadence

For the first two weeks, prefer **2–3 high-information posts per week** over daily filler.
The account begins with no audience history, so "best time" data is only a starting hypothesis.

Initial test window: weekday late morning Toronto time, especially around 10:00.
Change timing only after the account has enough impressions to compare like-for-like posts.

Do not optimize for follower count alone. Useful early signals:
- profile visits from qualified accounts,
- website taps,
- saves/shares on real-signal posts,
- replies/DMs from target businesses,
- trial or early-access requests,
- whether trial users return to the digest/feed.

## Outbound: primary early acquisition channel

Use a small, manually qualified batch before attempting volume.

A recipient is not "qualified" just because an email address exists. Before a commercial message,
record:
- company,
- recipient / role,
- why First Dig is relevant to that role,
- source URL where the business contact address was published,
- date checked,
- any statement against unsolicited messages,
- consent basis relied on,
- send date,
- unsubscribe status,
- outcome.

### Compliance gate

Do not send a commercial outreach email until all of the following are true:

- the sender identity is clear,
- the legal/business mailing address required for the message is configured,
- another valid contact method is included,
- the recipient has a documented consent basis appropriate to the message,
- the message is relevant to that person's business role,
- there is a simple unsubscribe mechanism,
- opt-outs are suppressed from future sends,
- the evidence used to justify the send is retained.

This gate exists because a high reply rate is not worth avoidable compliance risk.

### First experiment

Start with **5–10 individually researched prospects**, split across no more than two customer types.
Do not send the same generic paragraph to all of them.

A good first line references the prospect's actual service and one real First Dig record relevant to
that service. The email should be shorter than the explanation on the website.

Measure:
- delivered,
- reply,
- positive reply,
- demo/sample request,
- trial start,
- second-session / digest use,
- paid conversion.

The first batch is a learning experiment. If nobody cares, revise the wedge before increasing volume.

## Operating loop

Every morning:
1. refresh the filings;
2. generate the internal proof brief;
3. pick the strongest genuinely relevant record;
4. decide whether it is better used for a post, a specific outreach message, both, or neither;
5. log the action and result;
6. update the next experiment from observed behavior.

The system should become more selective as evidence accumulates, not simply produce more content.

# Name research: First Dig

Second pass, 2026-09-23. The first pick (EarlyFiled) was rejected for not flowing. This pass started from how the best companies in this exact category are named, derived the conventions, generated about 110 candidates against them, and bulk-checked every one against four domain extensions and the Canadian Trademarks Database.

## What works in this category

The comparables that own their names all follow one pattern: a **concrete noun from the customer's world, one or two syllables, stress on the first syllable, no descriptor**.

| Company | What it is | Why the name works |
|---|---|---|
| **Shovels** (permit data, US) | direct comparable | the tool that starts every job; "shovel-ready" |
| **Placer** (foot traffic) | location data | a surveying term, sounds like "place" |
| **Clay** (sales data) | enrichment | raw material you shape |
| **Dodge** (construction data) | 130-year incumbent | a surname that became a noun |
| **Hunter** (email finder) | prospecting | one verb, one image |
| **Cherre**, **Reonomy** | property data | coined; needed VC money to mean anything |

Two-part compounds work when both halves carry a stressed syllable and the pair makes a picture: BuildZoom, SafeGraph, PermitFlow, LoopNet. They fail when one half is a limp adjective or a participle, which is exactly what "Early Filed" was: a weak trochee plus a past participle, no image.

Conventions to avoid, per the 2026 naming reports: verb + "-ify/-ly", dropped vowels, "SomethingAI", anything with "Solutions" or "Data" in it.

## Availability wall

Every plain English noun in the construction and storefront vocabulary is registered on `.com` and `.ca`: shovel, lintel, hoarding, plumb, gable, docket, gazette, marquee, awning, herald, augur, bellwether, tremor, all of them. That is the same wall Shovels hit; they run on `.ai`. So the real choices were: a two-part compound with two strong syllables, or a plain word on `.io`/`.app`.

## Why First Dig

- **It is the moment the product sells.** The first dig is the first shovel in the ground, the thing every trade wants to hear about before anyone else does. It covers Opening Soon too: first dig into the filings.
- **It flows.** Two stressed monosyllables with hard consonants, like Shovels, Dodge, Clay. Say it on the phone once and it sticks. "Are you on First Dig?"
- **Clean register.** Zero results for "first dig" or "firstdig" in any field of the Canadian Trademarks Database. Web search finds no company, app or product by that name.
- **Room to grow.** Not tied to Toronto, to permits, or to one trade. Works for Vancouver, Calgary, Ottawa as feeds are added.
- **Domains.** `firstdig.app` is free through the Name.com Student Pack offer; `firstdig.ca` is about $12/yr and should be bought the same day; `firstdig.com` is on the aftermarket (typically $1,000 to $3,000) and is a later purchase, not a launch blocker.

Rejected from the shortlist: **Fresh Dirt** is the more vivid name and I nearly chose it, but "the dirt" means gossip, which cuts against trust on a B2B invoice, and a landscape architecture firm already trades as Fresh Dirt Design, uncomfortably close to our landscaping customers. **Sodturn** is clean everywhere but the two consonant clusters make it clumsy to say. **Augur** was the cleverest (a homophone of auger, the ground-drilling tool, and a seer who reads signs) but augur.com is a well-known prediction market.

Rejected earlier: FirstFiled (existing US company), PermitPulse (five companies), Toronto Signals (generic, .com taken), FiledFirst (UK business), EarlyFiled (doesn't flow).

## Before filing a trademark

The US register (USPTO) blocked automated queries from this machine. Do a manual search at tmsearch.uspto.gov for "first dig" before spending money on a Canadian or US application. A Canadian application is about $350 and can wait until there is revenue.

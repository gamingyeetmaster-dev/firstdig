How to find Toronto building permits and early construction leads

The City of Toronto publishes active building-permit records, but a single construction project can appear as several separate rows for demolition, plumbing, HVAC, drains and revisions. First Dig groups those filings into one project per address so contractors can search the signal instead of manually sorting the raw permit file.

What can a Toronto building permit tell you?

A permit can reveal the address, application timing, project description, stage, declared construction value and—in some filings—the applicant-stated builder. Those fields can help identify a teardown, new custom home, multiplex, garden suite or major addition before the downstream work is awarded.

Who uses construction-permit leads?

Pool builders, landscapers, fence and railing contractors, window and door companies, millwork shops, home-automation firms, movers, trades and other businesses can use permit timing to focus prospecting on properties where substantial work is actually planned.

Who uses it, and how

Pool and landscape

A teardown or new-house filing can mark the start of a long project sequence. Use the stage to research adjacent work that may still be unassigned; verify the recipient before outreach.

Fence, railing, deck

Filter to “Teardown”, “New house” and “Major addition” in your service area. Use project type, stage and applicant-declared value where it is present and usable; treat value as context, not a quote.

Windows, doors, millwork, closets

Stage helps separate newly applied work from issued or under-construction projects, so outreach can be timed to the part of the workflow that matters for your trade.

Realtors and lenders

Multiplex and garden-suite filings can be useful research signals for neighbourhood change, development activity and future financing conversations.

Trades looking for GCs

Some permit records include an applicant-stated builder. Where present, that field can support contractor and territory research; many records have no builder name.

Movers and storage

Major additions and underpinning flag disruptive residential work that can create adjacent moving or storage needs. Treat that as a hypothesis to verify, not a guaranteed job.

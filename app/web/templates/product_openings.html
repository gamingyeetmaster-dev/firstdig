How to find restaurants and businesses opening soon in Toronto

New businesses often leave public signals before opening day: a City business licence, an interior fit-out permit, or an AGCO liquor-licence application. First Dig joins those records by address so sales teams can identify likely openings without manually checking several databases.

Why opening-soon signals matter

Many purchases happen before the doors open: payments, insurance, food and beverage supply, signage, printing, uniforms, cleaning, waste, telecom and other setup services. Earlier visibility gives vendors time to reach a business while those decisions are still being made.

Three filings, one address

AGCO liquor application

Every new liquor sales licence goes on public notice for 21 days with the premises name and address. It can be a useful pre-opening signal for restaurants and bars, especially when another source points to the same address.

City business licence

Eating and drinking establishments, take-out, personal services, holistic centres, entertainment venues. Issued with the operating name, the licensee and sometimes a phone number.

Fit-out building permit

Interior alterations for “new restaurant (60 seats)”, “dental office fit-out”, “tenant fit-up Group E”. Comes with the declared construction budget.

Who buys from a business before it opens

  • POS, payments and merchant services — payments and POS decisions are often made before opening; the filing stage gives a reason to investigate timing.
  • Commercial insurance brokers — licensing and build-out activity can indicate that insurance decisions are approaching or underway.
  • Food, beverage and coffee distributors — pre-opening signals give suppliers a reason to research an account before normal operations begin.
  • Signage, printing, menus, uniforms, linen, hood cleaning, pest control, waste — these are common setup categories whose timing varies by business and stage.
  • Local media and delivery platforms — opening-stage businesses can have time-sensitive launch and onboarding needs; verify the stage before pitching.

First Dig organizes records published by public agencies. It does not append private contact data or scrape social media. Contact fields appear only when they are present in the underlying public source.
